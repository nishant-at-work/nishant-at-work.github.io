Riverfront development is being built across Indian cities on the template of Ahmedabad's Sabarmati — a project whose claims to environmental and social sustainability have not held up, and which effectively severed the river it was meant to restore. Patna is among the major cities on the Ganga where the same model is underway in phases.

This work examined that pattern through field study along the proposed Patna riverfront stretch, asking whether such projects bring a city closer to its river or displace the river altogether, and what happens to the cultivators, fishers and boatpeople whose livelihoods depend on an unengineered floodplain.

It extended to inland waterways, through a national consultation convened in New Delhi in January 2020 that brought together small-scale fish workers' platforms, researchers and river communities from the Ganga, Brahmaputra and the Sundarbans. Recurring concerns were the exclusion of fishing communities from navigational channels, damage to nets and boats, turbidity and its effect on breeding, and the near-total absence of public information about what was being planned.
