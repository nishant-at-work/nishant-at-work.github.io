Co-drafted with Akshita Rawat, with a working group drawn from Jawaharlal Nehru University, IIT Bhilai, Greenpeace India, the Centre for Policy Research, the Centre for Youth Culture, Law and Environment, and the Indo-Global Social Service Society.

Food and farming are treated as rural policy, so the farming that persists across Delhi's floodplains and urban villages — and the newer practices using rooftops and interstitial land — sits outside planning recognition, and therefore outside tenure security and public support. The draft set out to close that gap: to establish urban agriculture as a legitimate land use with a place in the master plan, and to attach to it the protections that recognition implies.

The policy was prepared in English and Hindi and circulated for public consultation. It drew on the [survey of urban farmers](/work/urban-agriculture-delhi/) and on case studies and thematic booklets produced alongside it, including work on farm labourers and on Delhi's fisherfolk.
